Mode: separate agent requests with software dice. All characters remain together. Private intentions and discoveries are excluded from this table-facing transcript.

## scene-0001

Ahead, the muddy lane leads unobstructed to the stone bridge, with water audible beneath its arch. Torren has offered to walk there together and ask Sella about Dain; his companions have yet to answer.

“I'm Sella. Are you here about the repairs?”

**Elara Voss**

GM: Sella listens to your questions, then opens the ledger and turns it so all three of you can see. She rests a finger beside an entry headed with yesterday's date: “Dain — morning — sluice clearing.”

“Someone gave that name for yesterday's morning shift,” she says. “I entered it when he reported for work. Whether he's the Dain you mean, I'd need a description. This records his arrival for the shift; it doesn't tell you when he left or where he went afterward.”

She keeps the page open. “For work today, I need loose stone sorted here on this bank. Two silver coins each for a full day's work, paid at the end. You wouldn't need to enter the water for that task, though the ground is wet.” Water continues to sound beneath the bridge as she waits for your reply.

## Round 3 — character turns 7–7

**Arlen Vale**

Attempt: Arlen studies the open ledger entry, then asks Sella for a description of Dain and a precise explanation of the stone-sorting task and day's terms.

GM: Sella leaves the ledger open while she answers Arlen. “The man who gave me that name was middling height, lean, perhaps thirty or thereabouts. Dark hair cut above his collar, and a pale scar through his left eyebrow. That's my recollection, not something written here. Does that sound like your man?”

She continues, “For you three, the two silver each would cover working from when you agree until sunset, with a meal break and pauses to rest. You'd separate sound loose stone from cracked pieces here on the near bank. Leave anything too heavy to lift comfortably for me to arrange help with. No wading, no sluice clearing, no climbing under the bridge. If I need different work, I'll ask separately; it isn't part of this offer. If you stop before sunset, we agree the pay for the time worked before you start. I won't promise that wet ground can't trip you.” She waits for your response, with the ledger still between you.
