# Tavern Zero — The First Night

Mode: single-model self-play with software dice. All three characters are together throughout this session. Private intentions and discoveries are excluded from this table-facing transcript.

## scene-0001

Rain ticks against the Crooked Lantern's shutters. Arlen Vale, Elara Voss, and Torren Bright share the dry end of a long table. A woman behind the counter sets down three cups. 'Mara Blackkeg. I run this place. Shut the door properly if you're staying; it sticks.' Somewhere below the floor, something gives a single, small metallic knock.

### Turn 1 — Arlen Vale

> Before I spend money I haven't got: anything here that needs fixing, finding, or discreetly ignoring?
Attempt: Arlen asks Mara about paying work while keeping an ear on the floor.

GM: Mara slides his cup closer. 'Find out why the cellar bell keeps ringing and your supper and beds are covered. It shouldn't ring. Nobody's down there.' Two sharper notes answer her from below. She does not look at the cellar door.

### Turn 2 — Elara Voss

> If someone's hurt, supper can wait. May we go down? And is there anything dangerous on the steps?
Attempt: Elara approaches the cellar door, asks permission, and checks the threshold before opening it.

GM: 'You may. Mind the bottom step,' Mara says. She takes a lamp from the counter and sets it beside Elara. The door opens on steep wooden stairs. A thin bell wire runs down the wall. Elara sees water glinting below but cannot see the whole cellar from here.

### Turn 3 — Torren Bright

> A wire before a haunting. That's encouraging. Please don't pull it yet.
Attempt: Torren examines the visible bell wire and its movement from the safe upper steps.

Dice — INVESTIGATION: [19]; selected 19 +2 = **21**.

GM: Torren rests his pencil beside the wire without touching it. The wire draws taut three times, pauses, then repeats. Each pull comes from below, not from the wind or the door. Its motion is visible to the three travelers on the upper steps; the pattern suggests a deliberate signal.

### Turn 4 — Arlen Vale

> Nobody down there, you said. Is that what you know, or what you were hoping?
Attempt: Arlen watches Mara's reaction while quietly questioning her claim about the cellar.

Dice — INSIGHT: [15]; selected 15 +3 = **18**.

GM: Mara folds the same corner of her cloth twice. 'I hoped the room was empty by now,' she says quietly. 'That's what I should have said.' She leaves the lamp where Elara can reach it.

### Turn 5 — Elara Voss

> Then let's not leave whoever it is waiting. I'll carry the lamp. Stay close, both of you; we don't know how deep that water is.
Attempt: Elara lifts Mara's lamp and waits on the upper steps for the others before leading a careful descent.

GM: The lamp throws a steady light down the stairwell. The bottom step is loose, just as Mara warned. From farther below comes a hoarse voice: 'Mind the rack. Don't move it from that side.' The speaker remains out of sight.

### Turn 6 — Torren Bright

> Arlen, a second pair of eyes? I'll mark the bad step. And no one touches the rack until we understand what's holding it.
Attempt: Torren waits alongside Elara and asks Arlen to join them, noting the loose step in his notebook.

GM: A second pull on the wire rings the bell. Then a scrape, followed by a sharp breath. The person below is awake and moving, but the party still has no view of what pins the rack.

### Turn 7 — Arlen Vale

> I'm coming. Mara, if there's another useful omission, now would be the economical time to spend it.
Attempt: Arlen joins Elara and Torren, then follows their agreed careful descent, avoiding the loose step.

GM: 'A traveler,' Mara says behind them. 'I let him sleep there. I thought he'd gone.' The three reach a broad landing together. Lamplight opens across shallow black water and rows of storage shelves. The voice comes from beyond a fallen rack.

## scene-0002

The cellar smells of wet wood. Water covers the floor to ankle height. Behind a tilted storage rack, a young traveler holds the bell wire in one hand; the other is trapped beneath a crossbar. A wooden drainage grate at the far wall trembles under a slow inward stream. The travelers remain together on the broad landing, within speaking distance of him.

### Turn 8 — Elara Voss

> I'm Elara. Tell me if this makes it worse. I'll lift just enough for your hand; don't try to stand until it's clear.
Attempt: Elara tests the rack's stable end, braces on the landing edge, and attempts a controlled lift to free the traveler's hand.

Dice — ATHLETICS: [11]; selected 11 +4 = **15**.

GM: Elara raises the corner by a few inches and holds it steady. The traveler pulls his bruised hand free and crawls onto the landing. 'Iven,' he says through his teeth. 'Thank you. I was trying to clear the drain. The rack shifted.' He keeps looking past them toward a small leather satchel caught under the grate.

### Turn 9 — Torren Bright

> Freeing him is one problem solved. The water is another. Iven, was that drain carrying water out before it started coming in?
Attempt: Torren studies the grate, water direction, and nearby floor marks from the landing without opening anything.

Dice — INVESTIGATION: [15]; selected 15 +2 = **17**.

GM: Torren locates a raised wooden shutoff board in grooves above the opening. The incoming water is pushing the satchel against the grate. Pulling the grate first would increase the flow; lowering the board first should cut it off. Iven nods at the grooves. 'That latch. I couldn't reach it without standing on the rack.'

### Turn 10 — Arlen Vale

> We have you. Before anyone fishes for that bag: is it worth a hand, or just worth money?
Attempt: Arlen asks Iven about the trapped satchel while looking for a safe route along the landing to the grate.

GM: 'Neither. Names,' Iven says. 'Receipts for repair wages. My brother's name is on one from last week. He hasn't been home in a month.' He points with his good hand. A narrow stone ledge runs from the landing to the grate; it offers footing, but the last arm's length is slick. 'If it goes, so does the only writing I've found.'

### Turn 11 — Elara Voss

> Then we save the names after we stop the water. I'll use the ledge. Nobody stands on that rack again.
Attempt: Elara moves along the stone ledge and tries to release the latch and lower the shutoff board under control.

Dice — ATHLETICS: [4]; selected 4 +4 = **8**.

GM: The latch releases, but the swollen board drops crookedly and jams halfway. Elara cannot force it farther from that footing. Water hisses through the remaining gap and drives the satchel harder into the grate. The waterline climbs over a chalky mark on the landing's lowest stone. Nobody is hurt, but the simple shutoff attempt has failed.

### Turn 12 — Torren Bright

> Don't pull harder from there. My staff can keep the board square. Arlen, could you hold this end? Elara, your rope could give you a pull from the dry step.
Attempt: Torren fits his walking staff against the board's accessible edge as a guide and proposes a three-person attempt using Elara's rope.

GM: The staff fits beside the crooked board and gives Torren a way to stop it twisting farther. It does not shut the water off by itself. There is enough room for another person to hold the guide while a rope is pulled from the landing. The water keeps rising while they arrange it.

### Turn 13 — Arlen Vale

> I prefer problems with handles. This one will do. Elara, on your count.
Attempt: Arlen joins Torren at the accessible end of the staff and steadies it against the board, preparing to guide it when Elara pulls.

GM: The two can keep the board aligned, but neither can supply the downward pull from that position. Iven moves his knees clear of the rope's intended path and holds the lamp steady on the landing. The guide is ready; the next pull will decide whether the board seats.

### Turn 14 — Elara Voss

> Ready. One, two—pull.
Attempt: Elara secures her rope to the board's handle and pulls from the dry step while Arlen and Torren keep it square with the staff.

Dice — ATHLETICS: [1, 6]; selected 6 +4 = **10**.

GM: The board shifts, then its rotten handle tears free with Elara's rope still around it. Arlen and Torren keep the staff from whipping loose, but the board remains jammed. There is no usable handle left. Water lifts the satchel's flap and a pale corner of paper streams out through the grate. The group has bought no reprieve; this repair now needs tools and footing they do not have.

### Turn 15 — Torren Bright

> Enough. We've rescued a person; let's not trade three more for a door. Iven, onto the upper step. Arlen, if you can reach it from the ledge, one try—then we leave.
Attempt: Torren withdraws his staff, offers it as support to Iven, and guides him toward the dry upper steps while keeping the lamp in view.

GM: Iven takes the offered staff with his good hand and climbs to the dry step beside the landing. He can stand and move without assistance once there. 'Leave it if you have to,' he says, although his eyes stay on the water. The satchel is still within one arm's reach of the ledge; its papers are not secure.

### Turn 16 — Arlen Vale

> One try. If I say let go, let go.
Attempt: Arlen crouches on the ledge and reaches for the satchel's shoulder strap, trying to lift the open bag without spilling its remaining papers.

Dice — DEXTERITY: [2]; selected 2 +3 = **5**.

GM: The wet strap parts in Arlen's hand. The bag turns under the grate and empties into the dark water beyond it. He has to brace both hands against the stone to keep his footing. When he can look again, the satchel and its papers are out of reach. Iven shuts his eyes. 'All right,' he says. 'All right. Come back.'

### Turn 17 — Elara Voss

> We're done down here. Iven first, Torren behind him. Arlen, come with me. Mara needs to know that board is broken.
Attempt: Elara gathers her rope and takes a position on the stairs to help Iven climb, leaving the broken board alone.

GM: Iven climbs past Elara using the sound steps. His hand is painful, but he can walk. The water continues below them. The broken handle comes away with Elara's rope; the board itself cannot be operated safely from the landing.

### Turn 18 — Torren Bright

> Mara! Your guest is coming up. He'll need clean cloth. And nobody else goes down until the water drops.
Attempt: Torren follows Iven to the upper steps with his recovered staff and calls to Mara for help.

GM: Mara appears at the top with folded linen. 'Iven?' She sets the cloth on the floor and reaches to steady him with both hands. Seeing the water on their clothes, she moves a chair away from the door to clear their path.

### Turn 19 — Arlen Vale

> A bell that rings because somebody needs help. I can invoice that as an investigation. The plumbing is beyond our qualifications.
Attempt: Arlen climbs back to the others, makes sure nobody remains below, and lets Mara know the satchel was lost.

GM: All three travelers and Iven reach the tavern floor. Mara closes the cellar door and draws a bench across it. Iven sits beside the counter, his empty good hand resting on his knee. The bell is silent below them.

## scene-0003

The four sit near the counter while Mara wraps Iven's hand in clean linen. Rain still brushes the shutters. The cellar remains flooded behind its blocked door, and the lost receipts remain lost. Mara places a pot of hot broth on the table. 'You found out what was ringing. Supper and beds, as promised.'

### Turn 20 — Elara Voss

> Thank you for the food. But next time, tell us someone might be down there. Why were you hiding him?
Attempt: Elara asks Mara directly about sheltering Iven while leaving him space to answer for himself.

GM: 'He asked for somewhere nobody would think to look,' Mara says. 'I gave him a dry corner. It was dry then. I didn't want strangers asking whose papers he had.' Iven nods. 'My request. Her mistake was thinking I left. Mine was trying to mend the drain alone.' Neither offers proof of who, if anyone, is looking for him.

### Turn 21 — Torren Bright

> I can write down what you remember. That won't replace the receipts. Your brother's name first, and the place printed on them. I'll mark this as your account, not proof.
Attempt: Torren opens his notebook and asks Iven for the exact names he remembers, distinguishing recollection from verified evidence.

GM: 'Dain,' Iven says. 'My brother is Dain. The heading was North Sluice Repairs. Seven days ago, wages collected beside his name. I didn't see him sign it. I don't know who did.' He stares at the page. 'I know how little that is without the paper.' Neither Mara nor anyone at the table can corroborate the receipt.

### Turn 22 — Arlen Vale

> We can ask about a man and a worksite tomorrow. That isn't a promise to accuse anyone, break into anything, or wade through another cellar. And if it becomes a job, we agree the price before the drowning.
Attempt: Arlen accepts Mara's supper and offers to help make cautious inquiries in the morning, with explicit limits.

GM: Iven gives a tired nod. 'Questions would be more than I had this morning.' Mara puts bread beside the broth. 'Ask in daylight,' she says. 'The drain can wait for someone with a proper brace.' No fee or route has yet been agreed.

### Turn 23 — Elara Voss

> Questions first. I want to know whether Dain needs help. Tonight we keep Iven warm and make sure nobody goes past that bench.
Attempt: Elara agrees to the morning inquiries, checks that Iven has a seat near the warmth, and leaves the cellar barrier in place.

GM: Mara brings another dry cloth for Iven's hand. He has stopped shivering by the time the broth cools enough to drink. The bench stays across the cellar door. Beyond the shutters, rain conceals whatever roads and settlements lie outside.

### Turn 24 — Torren Bright

> Dain. North Sluice Repairs. Claimed wages, not confirmed wages. Those are our questions. The rest can remain blank until morning.
Attempt: Torren reads back the distinctions in his notes, agrees to cautious inquiries at dawn, and closes the notebook for the night.

GM: Iven corrects nothing in the account. Mara lights the way to the promised beds. For tonight, the travelers have a rescued man, an unfixed flood, and two names written above a great deal of blank paper. The world beyond the tavern has not yet answered them.
